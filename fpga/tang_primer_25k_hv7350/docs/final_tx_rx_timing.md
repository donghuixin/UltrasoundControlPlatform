# Final Transmit Waveform and Receive-Window Control

> Historical eight-channel autostart behavior. See [four-probe control](four_probe_control.md)
> for the active five-second, one-probe-at-a-time firmware. The individual burst
> timing below remains valid; automatic startup and all-channel operation do not.

This document describes the final fixed FPGA build selected from the 2.0,
2.2, and 2.4 MHz damping measurements.

## Fixed FPGA Behavior

- FPGA clock: 50 MHz (20 ns per clock).
- Automatic startup: no button press is required after configuration.
- Carrier: fixed 2.2 MHz, NCO word `0x0B439581`.
- Main burst: 2 cycles, positive-unipolar return-to-zero (RTZ).
- Beam: fixed 0 degrees in this build; all eight built-in start delays are 0.
- PRF: 10 kHz; consecutive J11 rising edges and transmit bursts are 100 us
  apart.
- J11: 200 ns trigger pulse, exactly 2 us before each burst.
- J10: rises with the burst and is high for 50 us.
- Damping delay: 140 ns after each channel finishes its burst.
- Damping pulse: one 100 ns NIN pulse; with VNN=-30 V it commands a negative
  high-voltage pulse.
- S1 is disconnected. S2 is retained only as an optional emergency
  stop/restart input. READY and DONE are steady high while output is running.

The carrier and cycle count are hard-locked in the final RTL. UART packets
cannot change them.

## Exact Logic Timing Relative to J11

The ideal 2.2 MHz period is 454.545 ns. Because the output is generated by a
50 MHz NCO and its phase is reset at every burst, the actual repeated logic
pattern is quantized to 20 ns clocks:

| Time from J11 rise | PIN | NIN | Commanded HV7350 state |
| ---: | ---: | ---: | --- |
| 0 to 0.200 us | 0 | 0 | J11 high; TX remains at RGND |
| 0.200 to 2.000 us | 0 | 0 | Complete pretrigger interval at RGND |
| 2.000 to 2.240 us | 1 | 0 | First positive pulse, nominally +VPP |
| 2.240 to 2.460 us | 0 | 0 | RTZ interval at RGND |
| 2.460 to 2.700 us | 1 | 0 | Second positive pulse, nominally +VPP |
| 2.700 to 3.060 us | 0 | 0 | Final RTZ plus fixed 140 ns damping delay |
| 3.060 to 3.160 us | 0 | 1 | One negative damping pulse, nominally VNN |
| 3.160 to 100.000 us | 0 | 0 | RGND and receive interval |

The main NCO burst itself ends at approximately 2.920 us relative to J11:
240 ns positive, 220 ns RTZ, 240 ns positive, and 220 ns final half-cycle.
The fixed 140 ns damping wait then extends the zero state to 3.060 us.

These are digital command times. The measured ceramic voltage on CH2 is not an
ideal three-level waveform because the piezoelectric element, cabling, output
impedance, and acoustic stack form a resonant network. With VPP=+30 V and
VNN=-30 V, high-resolution captures showed instantaneous negative excursions
around -46 to -51 V. The supply voltage is therefore not a clamp on the
ceramic-terminal waveform.

## Echo Timing for 13-20 mm

For soft tissue sound speed `c = 1540 m/s`, two-way echo delay after acoustic
transmit is

```text
t_echo = 2 * depth / c
```

The center of the two-cycle transmit burst is approximately 2.46 us after
J11. Using that as the acoustic time origin:

| Depth | Two-way propagation | Approximate arrival after J11 |
| ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 13 mm | 16.88 us | 19.34 us |
| 20 mm | 25.97 us | 28.43 us |

A convenient conversion for an ADC timestamp measured from J11 is

```text
depth_mm approximately 0.77 * (time_us_from_J11 - 2.46)
```

The useful 13-20 mm range gate is therefore approximately 19.3-28.4 us after
J11. Keep a small implementation margin when defining sample indices.

## Receive-Side Ringing Protection

### 1. Prevent analog saturation first

Digital processing cannot recover samples acquired while the T/R switch or AFE
input is saturated. Use the T/R switch and input clamps as the primary
protection. Hold the AFE at its minimum gain, or use its available receive
blanking/power control, through the transmit and early ring-down interval.
Increase gain only after the input has returned to its linear range.

CH1 is measured after the T/R switch and therefore includes ceramic ringing,
T/R-switch feedthrough, clamp recovery, and AFE recovery. It is the correct
system-level node for deciding when useful acquisition can begin.

### 2. Use deterministic time blanking

- Trigger acquisition from the J11 rising edge.
- Mark 0-3.16 us after J11 as transmit/damping time and never use it as echo
  data.
- For the present 13-20 mm target, discard or blank all samples before about
  19 us after J11. The first useful range bin begins near 19.34 us.
- Continue acquiring through at least 28.5 us after J11 for the 20 mm boundary.

The measured residual around this range was near the receive noise floor for
the selected 2.2 MHz/140 ns setting. If hardware gain changes, repeat the
saturation and recovery check before trusting this gate.

### 3. Do not use an excessively narrow receive filter

Apply a moderate band-pass around the measured transducer band. A very narrow,
high-Q digital or analog filter creates its own long impulse response and can
turn a short feedthrough pulse into additional ringing. Preserve enough
bandwidth for the two-cycle echo, then use a matched filter or correlation with
the measured transmit/echo template if additional SNR is needed.

### 4. Remove repeatable residuals in slow time

The FPGA uses the same frequency, phase reset, damping delay, and J11 timing on
every 10 kHz frame. Residual ring-down is therefore highly coherent and appears
as stationary clutter across the Doppler ensemble. After range gating:

1. subtract a per-channel fixed ring/background template acquired without flow,
   or subtract the ensemble mean at each range sample;
2. apply a slow-time wall/clutter filter before Doppler estimation;
3. retain the same blanking indices and damping parameters for the complete
   ensemble so that subtraction remains coherent.

Template subtraction is not a replacement for analog protection. Do not use it
on clipped samples.

### 5. Recommended processing order

```text
J11 alignment
-> ADC scaling and saturation flagging
-> fixed early-time blanking
-> moderate receive band-pass
-> 19.3-28.4 us range gate for 13-20 mm
-> fixed-ring / ensemble-mean subtraction
-> slow-time clutter filtering
-> IQ demodulation or Doppler estimator
```

Keep a saturation-quality flag for every firing. Reject the complete firing if
the AFE remains clipped at the beginning of the useful range gate.
